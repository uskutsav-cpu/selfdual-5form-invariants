# Cross-dimensional stress-flow theorem: attempt and outcome

## Outcome, stated first

**A cross-dimensional theorem for the full stress-*flow* sector was NOT proven.**

What was proven is a weaker, precise statement about the stress-tensor **trace**
subring, together with an exact computation of the flow sector at `D = 10`. The
gap between the two is stated explicitly below rather than papered over, because
the temptation to present the trace bound as a flow theorem is exactly the error
this document exists to avoid.

## Proposition (trace-sector deficiency)

*Let `D = 4k+2`, let `V_D` be the self-dual `(2k+1)`-form module and
`G_D = SO(1,D-1)`. Assume the generic stabiliser of `V_D` is trivial. Then the
number of functionally independent invariants is `N_D = dim V_D − dim g_D`, while
the subring generated by traces of*
`M_mu{}^nu = F_{mu a_1...a_{2k}} F^{nu a_1...a_{2k}}`
*supplies at most `D − 1` functionally independent invariants. The functional
deficiency of the trace sector is therefore at least*

    N_D − (D − 1).

**Proof.** `M` is symmetric and traceless, hence a point of the space of
symmetric traceless `D x D` matrices, whose `G_D`-invariants are generated by the
`D − 1` independent eigenvalues, equivalently by `Tr M^n` for `n = 2, ..., D`.
Any invariant built from `M` alone is a function of those, so the trace subring
has transcendence degree at most `D − 1`. With trivial generic stabiliser the
generic orbit has dimension `dim g_D` and `N_D = dim V_D − dim g_D`. Subtract. ∎

**At `D = 10`:** `N_10 = 126 − 45 = 81`, `D − 1 = 9`, deficiency at least **72**.

**At `D = 6`:** the stabiliser is not trivial and the hypothesis fails; direct
count gives `N_6 = 2 ≤ 5`, so no deficiency. The proposition explains the
contrast without needing `D = 6` to satisfy its hypothesis.

## Exact graded confirmation at `D = 10`

Computed exactly at two primes on a common sample registry
(`results/stress_flow/trace_sector.json`):

| degree | `M`-trace sector | full graded space | codimension |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 1 | 1 | **0** |
| 6 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 8 | 2 | 7 | 5 |
| 10 | **2** | **14** | **12** |

The trace sector spans the whole space at degree 4 and then falls behind
immediately and fast.

## The gap: trace sector versus flow sector

This is the crux, and it is a limitation, not a result.

At degree 10 the two numbers are:

    M-trace sector:              dimension 2  of 14   (codimension 12)
    stress-flow-reachable D10:   dimension 11 of 14   (codimension 3)

So the flow reaches **nine dimensions beyond** the `M`-traces. The Proposition
bounds the trace sector, and therefore does **not** bound the flow sector. A
cross-dimensional theorem about flows needs a bound on what the flow adds, and we
do not have one.

Concretely, the missing ingredient is a `D`-independent bound on
`dim D_{D,n} − dim(M-trace sector at degree n)`. At `D = 10`, degree 10, that
difference is 9. Whether it is bounded by something like `dim g_D`, or grows with
`dim V_D`, is open.

## Is `Q_10 = 3` predicted, first, or accidental?

Four possibilities were considered. The evidence distinguishes some of them:

| possibility | status |
|---|---|
| predicted by the Proposition | **No.** The Proposition bounds the trace sector (codimension ≥ 12), not the flow sector (codimension 3). It cannot predict 3. |
| the first nonzero obstruction in this grading | **Not established.** Degrees 4, 6, 8 would have to be checked against the *flow* sector, not the trace sector, and degree 12 is unclassified. |
| a lower bound on an all-orders obstruction | **Not established.** Nothing here constrains higher degrees. |
| a finite-degree accident | **Not excluded.** |

Honest summary: `Q_10 = 3` is an exact fact at degree 10 and is not currently
explained by any theorem, including the one proven here.

## Degree 12: what it would decide, and why it was not run

Degree 12 is the natural discriminator. The competing hypotheses make different
predictions:

- if the flow deficiency is bounded, `dim Q_12` stays near 3;
- if it grows with the graded dimension, `dim Q_12` grows substantially, since
  the degree-12 graded space is far larger than 14;
- if degree 10 is accidental, `dim Q_12` could be 0.

This is a clean experiment. It was **not run**, for a stated reason rather than
by omission: the degree-12 graded space requires the reachable sector `D_12` to
be computed, which requires the flow closure at degree 12, and that computation
is the one this project has repeatedly found to be the expensive step. The
terminal-goal instruction permits bounded degree-12 work only where scientifically
required; here it *is* required to decide the theorem, and it is beyond the
compute budget that remained. That is a resource limit, and it is recorded as
such rather than as a scientific conclusion.

## Classification of this outcome

Outcome **D** — the finite-degree result is complete and exact; the
cross-dimensional theorem for the flow sector remains open, with a proven partial
result for the trace sector and a specific, formulated open question.

The natural universality conjecture — *"stress-tensor flows generate all local
interactions"* — is **false at `D = 10`**, and that was already known
qualitatively in the literature. What is added here is the exact codimension (3)
and the exact trace-sector codimension (12), which separates two statements that
had not been separated.
