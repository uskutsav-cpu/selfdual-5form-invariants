# Stress-flow framework: definitions

Written before any theorem is attempted, because most of the confusion in this
area comes from four different objects sharing one name.

## Setting

For `D = 4k+2` a self-dual `(2k+1)`-form field strength exists. The relevant
cases are `D = 2` (chiral scalar), `D = 6` (chiral two-form), `D = 10` (chiral
four-form). Write

- `V_D` — the self-dual `(2k+1)`-form module, `dim V_D = (1/2) C(D, D/2)`;
- `G_D = SO(1,D-1)`, `dim g_D = D(D-1)/2`;
- `A_{D,n}` — Lorentz-invariant polynomials of degree `n` in the components;
- `A_D = ⊕_n A_{D,n}`.

## Four distinct objects, kept apart

**1. Functionally independent invariants.** `N_D`, the transcendence degree of
the invariant field. When the generic stabiliser is trivial,
`N_D = dim V_D − dim g_D`. This is *cumulative over all degrees* and is a
statement about functions, not polynomials.

**2. The `M`-trace subring.** `M_mu{}^nu = F_{mu abcd} F^{nu abcd}` is symmetric
and traceless, so a `D x D` symmetric traceless matrix, with `D − 1` independent
eigenvalues. Hence `Tr M^n` for `n = 2, ..., D` — **at most `D − 1` functionally
independent invariants, for any `D`**. This is the "stress-tensor invariants" in
the narrow sense.

**3. The stress-flow-reachable sector `D_{D,n}`.** The span of degree-`n`
invariants generated by the stress-tensor flow from the free seed theory. This is
**larger** than the `M`-trace subring: the flow uses the full stress tensor, not
only traces of `M`. Conflating (2) and (3) overstates the obstruction, and this
document exists partly to stop that.

**4. The obstruction quotient.** `Q_{D,n} = A_{D,n} / D_{D,n}`. Graded, not
cumulative.

## What is NOT assumed

`D_D` is **not** asserted to be a ring, a subring, a module, or closed under any
differential operation. It is a span, degree by degree, and every statement below
treats it as such. Where closure was needed it was computed, not assumed.

## Why `D = 6` and `D = 10` differ, at the level of counting

| | `dim V_D` | `dim g_D` | generic stabiliser | `N_D` | `M`-traces available |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `D = 6` | 10 | 15 | positive-dimensional | 2 | ≤ 5 |
| `D = 10` | 126 | 45 | trivial | **81** | ≤ **9** |

In `D = 6` the group is *larger* than the module. The action is nearly
transitive, only two invariants exist, and the `M`-traces have more than enough
room to cover them. In `D = 10` the module dwarfs the group: 81 invariants exist
and `M` can supply at most 9, whatever one does with them.

That asymmetry is the mechanism, and it is dimension-counting, not an accident of
`D = 10`.
