The previous supportable statement was `dim_Q Q10 <= 3`. It is now an equality.

## Why the old statement was only a bound

The closure admits a generated flow direction when it raises the rank **modulo
`p`**. A direction rejected mod `p` still lies in `D10` and could be independent
over `Q`, so `dim_{F_p} D10 = 11` bounded `dim_Q D10` only from below — and
therefore bounded `dim_Q Q10 = 14 - dim_Q D10` only from above.

## What was done

Running the same fixed point in exact rational arithmetic removes the modular
admission test entirely. At the fixed point no generated direction raises the
rank **over `Q`**, so the reached span *is* `D10` and its rank is an equality.
No separate upper-bound argument is required; the upper bound was an artefact of
computing in `F_p`, not a gap in the mathematics.

The obstacle was that the flow targets are not all integral. Of the 58 targets at
degrees 4 through 10, **49 have identical integer coordinates at every prime** and
**9 are reductions of rationals**. Those nine were lifted by

1. CRT across five fitting primes `32693, 32713, 32717, 32719, 32749`;
2. rational reconstruction of each entry (Wang's algorithm, with the standard
   `sqrt(M/2)` bound and a coprimality check);
3. **validation against a held-out sixth prime `32771`** — every reconstructed
   entry was required to reduce correctly there.

Zero lifts failed and zero held-out reductions mismatched. The held-out prime is
what makes this a check rather than an assumption: a wrong lift consistent with
the five fitting primes would almost certainly disagree at the sixth.

The closure then ran to its fixed point in `Fraction` arithmetic, reaching it in
three sweeps, with rank by exact reduced row echelon form:

| degree | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `dim_Q` of the reached span | 1 | 2 | 7 | **11** |

These agree with the modular dimensions at every degree, which is corroboration
and not the argument.
