## Result

    dim_Q B10          = 12
    dim_Q P10          =  2
    dim_Q (B10 + P10)  = 13

The previous supportable statement was `dim_Q(B10 cap P10) <= 1`. It is now an
equality, and the intersection is exhibited explicitly.

## The explicit generator

The intersection is one-dimensional and generated by an integer identity among
the published candidates:

    -55 P10_01 - 252 P10_02 + 4200 P10_05 + 5040 P10_06 = -1040 (I4_1 * I6_1)

That is: a specific integer combination of four of the twelve published
degree-ten structures is exactly a multiple of the product of the degree-four and
degree-six invariants. This is what "the published span contains one product
direction" means, written out.

**Verified independently** at prime `32869` — used nowhere in the lift or its
holdout — on six freshly drawn self-dual five-forms. All six match.

## Method

`P10` is spanned by two coordinate directions and is exact by construction. What
had to be lifted was `B10`: the atlas coordinates of the twelve published
candidates, which come from solving a linear system modulo `p`.

1. Solve for the coordinates at fourteen primes.
2. CRT across seven fitting primes, then rational-reconstruct each entry.
3. Validate every reconstruction at a held-out prime `32783`.
4. Exact rational row reduction for the three ranks.

## Why six primes were not enough

The first attempt used five fitting primes and lifted only 9 of 12. The three
failures were `P10_10`, `P10_11`, `P10_12` — which are exactly the three
`AMB-02` candidates recorded in `docs/Q10_CANONICALITY_SCOPE.md`.

The correspondence is worth recording but is **not established as causal**:
`AMB-02` is a bracket-association ambiguity, whereas the lift failure is a height
problem. The plausible common cause is that the nested five-`N1050` structures
are the most deeply nested expressions and so carry the largest-height rational
coordinates. Adding primes fixed it, which is what a height problem predicts and
an ambiguity problem would not.

At seven fitting primes all twelve lift and the held-out prime confirms every
one.

## A wrong verification, recorded

The first check of the generator **failed** at a fresh prime, on all six samples.
The lift was not at fault: tested individually, all twelve lifted coordinate
vectors reproduce their candidates at a fresh prime with fresh samples.

The fault was in the check. It compared a published-candidate combination against
an atlas combination that had been separately cleared to integers and reduced by
its gcd — so the two sides differed by a constant factor. Recomputing with both
sides on one scale gives the identity above.

This is recorded because a failing verification is exactly the moment to
distinguish "the result is wrong" from "the check is wrong", and the per-candidate
test is what separated them. A check that fails and is quietly adjusted until it
passes is worthless.

## Consequence for the paper

The claim that the two `12 + 2` decompositions are not the same decomposition —
the correction this project reports most prominently — is now exact over the
rationals rather than modular. It was previously the claim most in need of a
characteristic-zero check, because a bad prime could only have weakened it.
