\section{The orientation-fixed finite-field bridge}
\label{app:orientation}

This appendix records the construction of the null frame over $\Fp$, the
square-root branch that it leaves free, and the regression that exposed the
consequences of leaving it free.

\paragraph{The frame congruence.}
The oscillator basis diagonalises the metric into $5$ hyperbolic pairs. Passing
to it requires a matrix $L$ with
\begin{equation}
L^{T}\,\eta\,L \;=\; \eta_{\mathrm{null}},
\label{eq:congruence}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\mathrm{null}}$ is the off-diagonal pairing of the null frame. Over
$\mathbb{R}$ one fixes $L$ by continuity from the identity. Over $\Fp$ there is
no such notion, and \eqref{eq:congruence} determines $L$ only up to the choices
made when extracting square roots.

\paragraph{The determinant character.}
Any two solutions of \eqref{eq:congruence} differ by an element of the orthogonal
group of $\eta$, which has two components. The component is detected by
$\det L$, and the two branches of the square root land in different components.
This is the entire mechanism: the branch choice is an orientation choice.

\paragraph{How the orientation propagates.}
An orientation reversal flips the sign of $\epsilon$, hence of $\star$, hence
exchanges the $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces. The gamma map of
appendix~\ref{app:bridge-exact} annihilates one eigenspace and is injective on
the other. Composing an orientation-reversed frame with the same gamma map
therefore yields the projector onto the \emph{anti}-self-dual space while every
variable in the program is still named as though it were the self-dual one.
Nothing raises an error; the first symptom is a downstream rank of zero where
$\dimSelfDualModule$ was expected.

\paragraph{The $p = 32707$ regression.}
Three cells of the rank matrix failed at $p = 32707$, at every seed. Two
observations show the prime is not exceptional:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Flipping the branch by hand at $p = 32707$ restores the expected rank.
\item Flipping the branch by hand at a prime that had been working breaks that
      prime in exactly the same way.
\end{enumerate}
The failure therefore tracks the branch, not the prime, and excluding the prime
would have concealed a defect that was live at every prime and merely latent at
most of them.

\paragraph{The fix.}
The frame routine now enumerates both branches, constructs the bridge with each,
and returns the one under which the self-dual space is the surviving one --- that
is, the branch for which the composite has rank $\dimSelfDualModule$. If neither
branch works it raises rather than returning a guess. The selection is
deterministic and depends only on $p$.

\paragraph{Independent verification.}
A dedicated test suite covers the fix. Beyond checking that all primes now
behave identically, it contains a test that deliberately selects the other branch
and asserts that the construction breaks. Without that test a future refactor
could drop the normalisation and the suite would still pass at most primes,
which is precisely the situation that produced the defect in the first place.

\paragraph{Effect on published values.}
None. Every certificate had been computed at a prime where the unpinned code
already selected correctly, and recomputation after the fix returned identical
values. The change is that the convention now holds by construction.
