# Referee A — invariant theory and representation theory

**A1. "Rank 81 is not your result."** Correct, and the manuscript says so. The
upper bound `126 − 45 = 81` follows from the trivial generic stabiliser, which is
cited. Only the lower bound is ours, and it is exact. *Accepted; already
reflected.*

**A2. "A modular rank proves nothing in characteristic zero."** In general, yes.
Here the Jacobian is the reduction of a genuine integer matrix, so
`det ≠ 0 mod p ⟹ det ≠ 0 over Z`. One prime suffices for the rank statement, and
an explicit `81 × 81` minor with `det = 20755 mod 32749` is frozen. *Answered by
certificate.*

**A3. "83 candidates, rank 81 — so you have 83 independent invariants?"** No, the
opposite: two functional dependencies exist among the selected functions. The
manuscript states this and a wording gate blocks the contrary phrasing.
*Answered.*

**A4. "Is your degree-10 basis complete, or just a spanning set you happened to
find?"** This is the strongest objection and it is only partly answerable. The
dimension 14 is a rank over `F_p` of an explicitly recorded evaluation matrix;
that part is certain. That the enumeration exhausts degree 10 in the declared
class rests on the search, corroborated by an independent formula-free reverse
recovery and by the spinor side, but it is not a symbolic proof. *Conceded and
stated in the End Matter.*

**A5. "Canonicality and minimality."** No canonicality is claimed; a wording gate
enforces it. Minimality is **removal** minimality within a fixed set, not
minimality under arbitrary basis change — the phrasing was tightened after this
objection was anticipated. *Answered; scope narrowed.*

**A6. "Quotient definitions."** `Q10 = A10/D10` where `D10` is the flow-reachable
span. `D10` is not claimed to be a ring or closed under anything; it is a span,
degree by degree. *Answered in the framework document.*
