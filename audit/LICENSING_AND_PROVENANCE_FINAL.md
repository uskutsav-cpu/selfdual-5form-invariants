## Licence: UNRESOLVED, and it blocks the release

There is **no licence file**. Without one:

- the code is not reusable by anyone, wherever it is hosted;
- the release must not be described as open-source software;
- the repository being already public does not change either point.

### What has to happen first

1. **Identify every copyright holder.** This is not decidable from the
   repository. It depends on the authorship decision, on whether the mentor's
   intellectual contribution to *this code* makes them a holder, and possibly on
   institutional policy for any employed contributor.
2. **Check for institutional constraints.** Some institutions require approval
   or claim ownership of research code.
3. **Obtain agreement from every holder.** A licence applied without one
   holder's agreement is not valid.

### Options, for the humans to choose between

| licence | practical difference here |
|---|---|
| **MIT** | shortest, most permissive, no patent grant, no attribution beyond the notice |
| **BSD-3-Clause** | as MIT plus a no-endorsement clause — useful if an institution's name appears |
| **Apache-2.0** | adds an express patent grant and a contribution clause; longer, and the usual choice when institutional lawyers are involved |

For a physics computation package with no patentable subject matter, MIT and
BSD-3-Clause are effectively equivalent in practice; Apache-2.0 matters mainly
if an employer requires an explicit patent grant.

**No selection is made here.** Choosing on behalf of undetermined copyright
holders would be exactly the kind of decision this audit exists to prevent.
